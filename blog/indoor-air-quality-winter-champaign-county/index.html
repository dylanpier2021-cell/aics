Indoor Air Quality in Winter: Cleaning Tips for Closed-Up Homes in Champaign County

Keeping your home dry, clean, and properly ventilated is one of the ideal ways to maintain healthy indoor air quality during winter in Champaign County.

When temperatures drop, opening windows becomes less appealing. That's why families tend to spend more time indoors, and heating systems run more frequently. As a result, dust, pet dander, cooking fumes, and other pollutants can gather over time. Even a home that looks spotless can have indoor air quality issues that go unnoticed.

For homeowners in Champaign County, where cold Illinois winters frequently mean keeping windows and doors shut for extended periods, regular cleaning becomes particularly important. Luckily, a few simple and practical changes to your winter cleaning routine can help reduce indoor pollutants and make your home more comfortable. Here's more about it.

Why Does Indoor Air Quality Get Worse in Winter?

During winter, homes receive less natural ventilation because windows and doors remain closed. Heating systems also circulate indoor air, potentially spreading dust and other airborne particles throughout the house.

According to the Environmental Protection Agency (EPA), people spend approximately 90% of their time indoors, making indoor air quality an important part of maintaining a healthy living environment. Common indoor pollutants during winter include:

  • Dust and dust mites trapped in carpets, rugs, and upholstery.
  • Pet hair and dander collected on furniture and bedding.
  • Mold and mildew caused by excess moisture.
  • Cooking fumes and household cleaning product residues.
  • Pollen and outdoor particles brought inside on shoes and clothing.

The good news is that regular cleaning and a few preventive measures can help manage many of these problems.

6 Winter Cleaning Tips to Improve Indoor Air Quality

A winter cleaning routine does not have to involve deep-cleaning your entire home every week. Concentrating on areas where dust, moisture, and allergens collect can make a noticeable difference.

1. Clean Carpets and Upholstery Regularly

Dust, pet dander, and other particles can accumulate in carpets, rugs, couches, and upholstered furniture. Some of the material may be released back into the air when you walk on these surfaces. If a vacuum with a HEPA filter is available, use it to clean high-traffic areas at least once or twice a week, paying special attention to living spaces, bedrooms, and entryways.

Consider hiring a professional carpet and upholstery cleaner for a more thorough clean. This helps get rid of accumulated grime that regular vacuuming could miss.

2. Replace HVAC Filters Before They Become Clogged

The quality of indoor air in the winter is significantly influenced by your heating system. A dirty furnace filter can limit airflow and lessen its capacity to collect airborne particles. Regularly inspect your HVAC filter and replace it as directed by the manufacturer. More frequent filter changes may be necessary in homes with pets or higher dust levels. Additionally, keep furniture, dust, and other obstacles away from supply vents and return air grilles.

3. Pay Attention to Kitchen and Bathroom Moisture

Moisture is introduced into indoor air through cooking, showering, and dishwashing, and its accumulation can promote the growth of mold and mildew. To prevent moisture-related problems:

  • Run the kitchen exhaust fan while cooking.
  • Use bathroom exhaust fans during and after showers.
  • Wipe condensation from windows and nearby surfaces.
  • Check under sinks for plumbing leaks.
  • Clean visible mildew promptly and address its moisture source.

The Illinois Department of Public Health recommends maintaining indoor relative humidity between 30% and 60%. A simple hygrometer can help you monitor moisture levels.

4. Dust Surfaces Using the Right Cleaning Method

Particles may be pushed into the air rather than removed by dry dusting with a regular cloth. Shelves, tables, window sills, ceiling fans, and other often neglected surfaces can be cleaned with a slightly moist microfiber cloth. Start with higher surfaces and work your way down so you can clear falling dust later. Don't forget places like:

  • Blinds and window frames.
  • Light fixtures and ceiling fans.
  • Baseboards and corners.
  • Behind and underneath furniture.
  • Electronics and decorative items.

5. Keep Entryways and Floors Clean

Road salt, mud, and other outdoor debris can enter your home through winter footwear. These substances can disperse throughout floors and create new indoor dust sources. Sweep or vacuum entryways on a regular basis, and mop hard floors with the proper cleaner. By adopting this small habit, you can lessen the amount of outside dirt that enters other rooms.

6. Allow Fresh Air Inside When Weather Permits

Keeping windows closed throughout winter can reduce natural ventilation, but that does not mean your home should remain completely sealed. On milder days, briefly open windows when outdoor air quality and weather conditions allow. Use kitchen and bathroom exhaust fans that vent outdoors to remove odors and excess moisture. Avoid bringing in outdoor air during periods of poor air quality or extreme cold. Proper ventilation should complement, not replace, regular cleaning.

These are practical starting points. Adjust the frequency according to household size, pets, and indoor conditions.

Don't Forget Carbon Monoxide and Radon

Not all indoor air quality issues can be resolved by cleaning alone. Heating equipment needs special care in the winter. Install functional carbon monoxide detectors on every floor of your house and near sleeping areas, and have fuel-burning equipment tested by a certified expert once a year.

Homeowners in Illinois are also concerned about radon. This naturally occurring gas cannot be detected without testing and can enter homes through foundation openings. The Illinois Department of Public Health recommends testing homes and addressing elevated radon levels.

Keep Your Champaign County Home Cleaner This Winter

Good indoor air quality begins with consistent cleaning, attention to hidden sources of moisture and dust, and proper ventilation. You don't need a long, elaborate routine to make a difference. Taking care of the floors, furniture, carpets, and heating system filters will make your home more comfortable and cleaner as you go.
